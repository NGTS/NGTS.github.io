The Next Generation Transit Survey (NGTS) has now been in operation for over a decade. Conceived as a successor to the hugely succesful Wide Angle Search for Planets (WASP), NGTS remains one of the best facilities in the world for ground-based exoplanet transit photometry. Our location at ESO Paranal and flexible system of twelve independent robotic 20cm telescopes fitted with custom red optimized CCD cameras from Andor Technology allow us to deliver world class photometric precision that rivals or even exceeds that of some space based facilities.

Since achieving first light in 2015, NGTS gone on to produce some of the most exciting discoveries in modern exoplanet science. This includes the `Monster Planet'; NGTS-1b, one of the first giant planets found around an M-dwarf star, the `Forbidden planet'; NGTS-4b, the smallest transiting planet ever detected from the ground and one of the first planets found in the so-called `Neptune desert' and some of the shortest-period `ultra-hot-Jupiters' such as NGTS-6b and NGTS-10b. With the advent of NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Sattelite (TESS), NGTS has transitioned from being a dedicated survey facility to providing photometric followup for the most exciting discoveries from TESS. In particular this has led to the confirmation of some of the longest period transiting planets, including systems such as NGTS-11b - the first exoplanet discovered from just a single TESS transit and more recently NGTS-38b - one of the longest period transiting planets ever discovered. Beyond exoplanets, NGTS has also been enabling cutting edge science in the fields of binary stars, stellar flares and other areas of time-domain astronomy.

To celebrate ten years of NGTS, we are hosting a special conference on the Isle of Skye in Scotland from the 24th to the 28th of May 2027. This conference aims celebrate the contributions of as many people as possible who have contributed to our success over the last decade. With the future uncertain from STFC budget cuts we also wish to use this meeting as an opportunity to dicsuss how the project may continue into the next decade and the lessons that can be learned from NGTS for future projects. We look forward to welcoming, consortium members, collaborators, old friends and new to Skye to celebrate the (first?) ten years of the Next Generation Transit Survey.
