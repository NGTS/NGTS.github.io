Things to do on Skye

Alongside the programme of LOC planned social events above, we have assembled a brief list of recommendations of things to do in your spare time on Skye, mainly focussed on the Sleat Peninsula around the venue. The island is full of spectacular scenery and rich in history, both natural and human. Do be aware that the conference will be taking place around the start of the tourist season and things will be busy so book in advance to avoid dissapointment

Torabhaig Whisky distillery

The Scottish highlands are famous for their whisky and Skye is no exception. Most pubs will stock local whiskys and some of the distilleries are open for tours and tastings (See this guide on how to drink Whisky like a sir). The famous Talisker distillery is located around an hour's drive north of the conference venue. Much closer, however, is the relatively new Torabhaig distillery, just 2.4 miles away. For more information see their website: torabhaig.com.

Armadale Castle

Armadale castle is the historic seat of Clan Donald and is located just 2 miles South of the conference venue. The castle itself is now in a state of managed ruin but the grounds also contain an extensive garden and the museum of the highlands, which covers history from the early Pictish, Gaelic and Norse to the Jacobite rebellions and highland clearances. The Stables cafe and bar on site also provides a nice place for lunch and a drink. For more information see their website: armadalecastle.com

Nature and Hiking

Natural beauty is everywhere on Skye and there are plenty of walking trails and sights worth seeing. The front desk of the conference venue have a folder of information on nearby walking trails available on request. The old man of Storr is one of Skye's most famous landmarks and is around 90 minutes away by car.

For wildlife enthusiasts, Skye contains some of the greatest biodiversity in the UK. Keen birders should keep an eye out for Ravens as well as golden and white-tailed eagles.

Dinosaurs and Fossils

Skye is host to some of the richest fossil bearing rocks in the UK and is especially famous for its trackways found on beaches like an Corran and Brother's point. The Staffin museum houses a small but impressive collection of local specimens and is worth a visit if you find the time to travel north.
